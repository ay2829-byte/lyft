Driver Quality Coach

A product brief for the Driver Quality PM role at Lyft.

The problem

  • Drivers see a number, not the story behind it. A driver's rating is the average of their last 100 rides, and third party guides say drivers can't tell who left a rating. Minimums are not published and are reported to vary by city.
  • When something goes wrong, the message is a dead end. Drivers describe "decision is final" notices that never say which reason applied. A Chicago driver told local news he was permanently deactivated over a complaint he says he never got to answer.
  • Regulators are paying attention. In New York, council members proposed independent review before a driver can be deactivated, and the taxi workers alliance says single complaints can end a driver's access.
  • Drivers are working around the product. A popular driver forum thread tells people to file arbitration after generic appeal denials. That is a sign the in app path is not trusted.

Goal

A driver whose quality slips should understand why in under a minute, know the one thing most worth changing, and get a fast, human answer when a flag is wrong. This makes drivers better at the job and makes the system fairer, in that order of effort.

What I would ship, in order

  1. Weekly coach, read only. Rating explained through the last 100 rides, what moved it, one recommendation. No policy changes, so it can launch quickly and teach us which explanations drivers act on.
  2. Ride level explanations. Each low rating shows the rider's tags and the driver's own trip data next to it.
  3. Dispute with evidence. Trip data attached automatically, driver adds a note or clip, a person reviews it inside a stated time, and the reason for the decision is written back.

How I would measure it

Success

  • Recovery rate: drivers below their market's quality bar who are back above it within six weeks
  • Support contacts per quality notice
  • Time from dispute to decision
  • Drivers who say they understand the reason behind a decision
  • Sixty day retention for drivers who received a notice

Guardrails for riders

  • Rider reported safety issues stay flat or improve
  • Average rider rating of drivers holds
  • Dispute overturn rate and repeat disputers stay in a healthy band, so the flow isn't gamed
  • Review queue stays inside its promised time
  • Coaching never nudges unsafe behavior, such as hurrying a pickup

The tradeoff I would watch

Making it easier to contest a rating could raise dispute volume and weaken rider trust. The design answer is that a dispute adds driver context and trip data. It does not erase rider feedback. The trip data should also cut reviewer time per case, which is what pays for the promise of a faster answer.

What I would need to learn first

  • Which quality signals actually trigger warnings today, and how much of that is ratings versus safety reports.
  • How often low ratings come with a fixable pattern, like pickup waits, versus rider mood.
  • What New York rules would require of any dispute process. This needs legal input before launch.

The prototype uses invented numbers and my own reading of public reports. It is a way to make the questions above concrete, not a claim about how Lyft's systems work.
